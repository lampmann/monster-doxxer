Name

If your DM used a name — even one you didn't recognise as a creature — put it here. Spelling doesn't matter; you heard it, you didn't read it. This is the strongest single thing you can tell the tool.

Traits

What it could do. This is the strongest evidence the tool has — far stronger than any stat. Search reads the descriptions as well as the names, so you can find a trait from what you watched it do without knowing what it is called. What kind of damage it dealt belongs in Attacks and saves below, where it can be tied to the attack that dealt it.

Appearance and lore

Your words, not the book's — and not only what it looked like. This is matched against each monster's whole description, so what it was counts too: where it lived, what it served, what the GM said about it. This can only ever help a monster's score — it never counts against one, because reflavouring is exactly the case where the description is wrong and the monster is right.

Attacks and saves

One row per thing it did — each row is matched against a single action on the statblock, so “a melee attack that burned me” finds creatures whose claw deals fire, not every dragon with a breath weapon. Fill in what you remember and leave the rest blank; a blank field costs a monster nothing.

Spellcasting

Name the spells you recognised — any spell, whether or not it is suggested. These are held loosely on purpose: a GM re-preparing a caster's spells is the most ordinary thing in the game, so a spell the statblock lacks barely counts against it. How it cast is a different matter — that comes from what the creature is, and it is weighed in full.
